How to read a Signal backup on a computer

Since Signal 8.30, the on-device backup is no longer a single file: it's an encrypted folder shared by Android, iPhone and Signal Desktop. Here's how to open it and read your messages without a phone, using your recovery key.

Open my backup with Lanterne

What a Signal local backup contains

The backup folder (often named SignalBackups) holds:

  • one dated subfolder per backup, with two files: main (every message, compressed then encrypted) and metadata (the backup's encrypted ID);
  • a shared files folder, split into two-character subfolders, holding photos, videos, voice notes and documents, each encrypted with its own key.

Everything is protected by your 64-character recovery key. Without it nobody can read the backup, not even Signal.

Read the backup in 4 steps

  1. Find your recovery key. Signal shows it under Settings, Backups if your phone still works; otherwise check the password manager where you saved it.
  2. Copy the whole folder to your computer (USB cable, SD card, or the folder you picked in Signal Desktop). Keep the files folder to get your media back.
  3. Open Lanterne, click "Choose folder" and select the parent folder (the one containing files).
  4. Paste the key and click "Decrypt and view". Your chats appear, with search and text export.

Lanterne decrypts everything inside your browser. To check, turn off your internet connection after opening the page: the tool keeps working.

Common problems

"Wrong key". The key doesn't match this backup. Check all 64 characters: # and = are part of it. If you generated a new key, only backups made afterwards open with it.

Photos don't show. You selected the dated subfolder instead of the parent folder. The files folder must be part of the selection.

I have a signal-2025-….backup file. That's the old Android format, protected by a 30-digit passphrase. Lanterne doesn't read it; the free tool signalbackup-tools does.

I only have a cloud backup (Secure Backups). It can only be restored inside the Signal app, during setup.

What about privacy?

Your recovery key is the master key to your backups. Never type it on a site someone sent you in a message. Lanterne is open source, has no account and no ads on the tool page, and a strict security policy stops the page from sending anything.
